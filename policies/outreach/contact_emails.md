## Australia — New South Wales, Teachers Re-Engage
**To:** NSW Department of Education, Teach NSW / school workforce
**Subject:** OECD research on returning teachers: a request to learn about Teachers Re-Engage

Dear [Name],

My name is [Name], and I am a Policy Analyst at the OECD Centre for Educational
Research and Innovation (CERI) in Paris. At CERI I work on a project called
Unlocking the Potential of Diverse Teaching Profiles, which examines routes into
the teaching profession beyond the so-called "traditional" pathway and how
education systems can use them to address teacher shortages. Our current line of
research documents policies that seek to bring retired teachers, and teachers who
have left the profession, back to the classroom.

In that context we have identified Teachers Re-Engage as one of the clearest
examples we have found anywhere. Rather than advertising vacancies, the
Department used its own staffing records to contact teachers who had resigned in
the previous five years, and it published the whole path from the teachers
contacted to those placed in schools. Very few systems report that openly.

Our understanding comes from the Department's media release and its Returning
teachers page, and we would like to hear directly how it has worked: how the idea
came about and how it was organised inside the Department, what you learned about
who responds to this kind of approach and who does not, what made settling back
into teaching easy or hard for those who returned, and what you would keep or
change today.

We would be delighted to arrange a short video call at a time that suits you and
your colleagues, and to tell you more about the project and how the insights we
gather from country experiences will be used.

Thank you very much for considering this request. Please do not hesitate to reach
out if you have any questions, and I would be very grateful for any indication of
when you might be available.

Warm regards,

**Subject:** OECD research on returning teachers: a request to learn about the herintreders grant

education systems can use them to address teacher shortages. Our current line of
research documents policies that seek to bring retired teachers, and teachers who
have left the profession, back to the classroom.

In that context the Dutch scheme for returners in primary education is unusual in
our collection, and that is why we would like to understand it properly: it paid
the school rather than the teacher, with the money earmarked for the returner's
guidance in the first months back. It is also one of the few measures that has run
its course, so the whole story can be told. The Dutch evidence on the number of
qualified teachers working outside education makes the question all the more
interesting.

We would like to hear directly from the ministry: the thinking behind paying the
school rather than the teacher and how schools used the money, what you learned
about the teachers who came back through the scheme and about those who stayed
away, why the scheme ended and what has taken its place, and what you would advise
a country considering something similar today.

We would be delighted to arrange a short video call at a time that suits you and
your colleagues, and to tell you more about the project and how the insights we

In that context Michigan is a case we would very much like to understand from the
inside. As we read it, the 2023 change shortened the wait before a school can
re-hire a retired employee and removed the cap on what they may earn afterwards,
with the pension continuing in full. Because the change has a clear before and
after, and because it is set to expire in 2028, Michigan is one of the few places
where the question "did this work?" will actually have to be answered.

We would like to hear directly from your office: the reasoning behind loosening
the rules and how the pension side and the schools side saw it, how districts and
retirees have used the new rules since they came in, whether you have seen

## Portugal — hiring retired teachers for shortage posts
**To:** Ministério da Educação, Direção-Geral da Administração Escolar, teacher recruitment
**Subject:** OECD research on returning teachers: a request to learn about the hiring of retired teachers

Dear [Name],

My name is [Name], and I am a Policy Analyst at the OECD Centre for Educational
Research and Innovation (CERI) in Paris. At CERI I work on a project called
Unlocking the Potential of Diverse Teaching Profiles, which examines routes into
the teaching profession beyond the so-called "traditional" pathway and how
education systems can use them to address teacher shortages. Our current line of
research documents policies that seek to bring retired teachers, and teachers who
have left the profession, back to the classroom.

In that context the Portuguese measure interests us for a reason that may seem
unusual: what it tells us about the limits of a financial incentive. As we
understand it, a retired teacher who returns to a shortage post keeps the full
pension and receives a supplement on top, and yet a good part of the posts opened
in the first round went unfilled. Most of the policies we have collected report
only their design; very few can say why the people they were meant for said no.

We would like to hear directly from the ministry: what led you to open these posts
to retired teachers and how the quota was agreed, how the first round went from
the posts offered to the teachers who actually started, what you know about why
retired teachers turned the offer down and what those who accepted were looking
for, and how the measure has been adjusted since.

We would be delighted to arrange a short video call at a time that suits you and
your colleagues, and to tell you more about the project and how the insights we
gather from country experiences will be used. We would be glad to hold the call in
Portuguese or Spanish if that is easier.

Thank you very much for considering this request, and I would be very grateful for
any indication of when you might be available.

Warm regards,
