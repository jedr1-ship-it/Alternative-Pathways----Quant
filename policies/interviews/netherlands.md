# Netherlands
## Regeling tegemoetkoming herintreders primair onderwijs (2017–2022)

**Who to ask for:** the primary-education directorate of the Ministry of
Education, Culture and Science, and the grant agency that handled the
applications (DUS-I).

**What we already know, so we do not ask it.** The regulation took effect on
1 November 2017 and paid a primary-school board up to 2,500 euros for each
qualified teacher it took on who had not worked in primary education for at least
a year, on a contract of six months or more. The money went to the board, not the
teacher, and was meant for the guidance and support the returner needs in the
first months. The first budget was 1.25 million euros, enough for about 500
returners; a 2019 amendment extended the scheme, applications were accepted until
1 November 2020 and the regulation expired on 1 January 2022. The ministry
reported in mid-2019 that more than a hundred returners had used it, against a
pool of qualified teachers outside education that the ministry itself put in the
tens of thousands.

---

### Opening

1. Could you tell us how the scheme came about in 2017, and what the thinking was
   in paying the school rather than the teacher?

2. Was that design debated at the time? We are interested in what the alternative
   would have been.

### How it worked

3. What did a school board actually have to do to claim it, and what did they
   spend the money on?

4. Did 2,500 euros change a school's decision, or did boards hire the person
   anyway and claim afterwards?

5. Looking at the take-up, it seems that a good deal less of the money was used
   than was available. What was behind that?

6. In 2019 the scheme was extended rather than dropped. What was the argument for
   extending it, and what changed afterwards?

7. It ran out at the beginning of 2022 without being replaced by anything of the
   same kind, and the work moved into the regional partnerships. How did that
   decision come about?

### The teachers

8. Who are the herintreders, in your experience? We are aware the definition also
   covers people who qualified and never started.

9. What do they say about why they left primary teaching, and about what makes
   coming back possible?

10. A Dutch teaching qualification does not expire, so there is no re-licensing
    hurdle. Does that make returning easier than in other systems, or does it
    simply move the difficulty somewhere else?

11. The number of returners in primary education has fallen over the last decade
    while the shortage has continued. How do you read that?

### Results and what comes next

12. Do you know whether the teachers who came back through the scheme stayed?

13. Was it ever evaluated, and what would you say it taught you?

14. The debate about the silent reserve now seems to be about whether that pool
    is really available, given how many are already working elsewhere. Where has
    the ministry landed?

### Close

15. Is there a colleague at the ministry, or at the agency that handled the
    applications, who followed the scheme closely and could speak to its
    experience and its results? We would be grateful if you could point us to
    them.
