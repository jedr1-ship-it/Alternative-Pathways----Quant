# Australia — Victoria
## Teacher Re-Engagement Initiative (TREI), Department of Education (since late 2022)

**Who to ask for:** the re-engagement team in the Department of Education, in
the area responsible for attracting teachers to government schools.

**What we already know, so we do not ask it.** The initiative began in late 2022
as a call for expressions of interest, which more than 1,100 teachers answered,
and the money followed: the 2023/24 State Budget put 12 million dollars into paid
placements of up to forty days in government schools, with placements to be
completed by June 2025. A teacher who has been away from a fixed-term or ongoing
role for a year or more registers, the team calls them, coaching is offered on
applications and the current curriculum, and a placement is arranged with a
serving teacher alongside; those returning from a career break or retirement
receive an honorarium of up to 12,000 dollars. Teachers returning from more than
two years of parental absence are on a different footing: a temporary resumption
of duty on their own salary. As far as we can tell from the current departmental
pages, the placement is now described as school-funded, at 330 dollars a day. No
figure has been published on placements made or on how many went on to a post.

---

1. **How did the idea come about?** We understand the expressions of interest
   came first and the budget afterwards, which is the reverse of the usual
   order.
   - What was the thinking in paying someone to try the classroom before they
     decide, rather than asking them to decide first?
   - What else was on the table at the time?

2. **Could you walk us through what happens from the moment a teacher registers
   to the moment they walk into a school?**
   - What does the call from the team actually cover?
   - How are teachers and schools matched, and what happens to someone who
     cannot be placed?

3. **What does a school take on when it hosts a returner, and what does it get
   out of it?**
   - What does the serving teacher alongside them actually do, and how is that
     found inside the timetable?
   - Is the forty days a fixed shape, or does it become something different for
     each person?

4. **We noticed the placement is now described as funded by the school rather
   than centrally. Is that right, and if so, how did it come about?**
   - What changed in schools' willingness to host once they were paying?

5. **Who came forward?** Was it the people you expected?
   - Of the things on offer — the paid days, the coaching, the mentor, the way
     back into a school — which one do people actually come for?
   - Teachers returning from parental leave are treated differently from
     retirees and career-break returners. How did that come about, and does it
     work?

6. **When you think about why teachers leave Victorian schools, are the ones who
   came back the same people, or a different group?** This is the question our
   chapter turns on.
   - What did they tell you about why they had left, and what worried them most
     about coming back?
   - Does a paid re-entry reach the teacher who left because of the job itself?

7. **What happened after the placements?** We have not seen a figure anywhere.
   - Who took a post, who did not, and what became of those who decided the
     classroom was not for them after all?
   - Has any of it been evaluated, and what did the cost look like against what
     it produced?

8. **Where does the initiative stand now, and what would you change if you were
   designing it again?**

9. **Is there a colleague who has run this closely and could speak to the
   experience and the results?** We would be very grateful for an introduction.
