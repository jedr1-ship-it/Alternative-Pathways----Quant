# Australia — Victoria
## Teacher Re-Engagement Initiative (TREI), Department of Education (since late 2022)

**Who to ask for:** the team in the Department of Education that runs the
Teacher Re-Engagement Initiative, in the area responsible for attracting
teachers to government schools.

**What we already know, so we do not ask it.** The initiative began in late 2022
as a call for expressions of interest, which more than 1,100 teachers answered,
and the money followed: the 2023/24 State Budget put 12 million dollars into paid
placements of up to forty days, with placements to be completed by June 2025.
A teacher who has been away from a fixed-term or ongoing role for a year or more
registers, the re-engagement team calls them, and a placement is arranged in a
government school with a serving teacher alongside; those returning from a career
break or retirement receive an honorarium of up to 12,000 dollars. Teachers
returning from more than two years of parental absence are on a different
footing: a temporary resumption of duty on their own salary. The current
departmental pages describe the placement as school-funded at 330 dollars a day.

---

### Opening

1. How did the idea come about? We understand the expressions of interest came
   first and the budget afterwards, which is unusual — could you tell us how that
   happened?

2. Paying someone to spend time in a classroom before they decide is a real
   choice of design. What was the thinking behind it, and what else was on the
   table at the time?

### How it works

3. Could you walk us through what happens from the moment a teacher registers to
   the moment they walk into a school?

4. How are teachers and schools matched, and what does a school take on when it
   hosts someone? What do principals get out of it, and what does it cost them?

5. What does the serving teacher alongside the returner actually do, and how is
   that arranged inside a school's own timetable?

6. Is the forty days a fixed shape, or does it turn into something different for
   each person?

7. We noticed the placement is now described as funded by the school rather than
   centrally. Could you tell us how that came about and what it changed?

### The teachers

8. Who came forward, and was it the people you expected?

9. What did they tell you about why they had left, and about what worried them
   most about coming back?

10. Of the things on offer — the paid days, the coaching, the mentor, the way
    back into a school — which one do people actually come for?

11. Teachers returning from parental leave are treated differently from retirees
    and career changers. How did that distinction come about, and does it work?

### Results and what comes next

12. What happened after the placements: who took a post, who did not, and what
    happened to those who decided the classroom was not for them after all?

13. Has any of this been evaluated, and what did you conclude about what it cost
    against what it produced?

14. Where does the initiative stand now, and what would you change if you were
    designing it again?

### Close

15. Is there a colleague who has run this closely and could speak to its
    experience and its results? We would be very grateful for an introduction.
