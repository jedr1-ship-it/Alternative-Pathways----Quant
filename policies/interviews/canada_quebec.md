# Canada — Quebec
## Measure 15178, pay for returning retired teachers, since 2020

**Who to ask:** someone in the direction of the Ministère de l'Éducation
responsible for teaching staff and for the budget rules of the centres de
services scolaires.

**What we know:** A mandate (measure 15178) allowed retired teachers who cover
absences to be paid at the salary step and experience held at retirement and
continue receiving their pension. Normally a supply teacher, whatever their
experience, is paid a flat daily rate and only reaches their own step after
twenty consecutive days in the same replacement. So, for a retiree at the top
of the scale, the measure roughly doubled the daily rate on top of the pension.
The ministry pays for it through the yearly budget it gives to the school
service centres, which run the public schools. Because it pays supply teachers
more than what the pay rules negotiated with the unions allow, the unions must
sign it separately, and they do so every school year. It was announced in
September 2020 for teachers retired since 2015, extended to all retirees in
January 2022 and made permanent the same month. In 2024-25 the ministry counted
more than 5,000 retirees back in schools. What we do not know is how that figure
is counted, whether the measure has been evaluated, and whether it is true, as
the press reported in December 2025, that some school service centres call
retirees last because they cost more.

---

1. **In what context did this measure come about in 2020, and with what
   purpose?** We understand it was a pandemic response; why a pay rule, rather
   than a programme, a campaign or a change to pension rules?

2. **We understand the measure is permanent on the budget side, while the
   agreement carrying it is renewed each year with the unions. How does that
   work in practice?**
   - What is renegotiated, and what has changed from one year to the next?

3. **From a school service centre's perspective, how does the measure work?**
   - How do they draw on it, and what do they have to report?
   - According to the press, some centres call retirees last because they cost
     more. Is this accurate?

4. **How would you describe the retirees who come back, and their
   obligations?**

5. **We understand that in the context of this program the minister has written
   to retired teachers twice. What came of those letters? Can you explain their
   purpose and impact?**
   - Do you know what retirees say when they decline?

6. **Is there any record of what the measure has changed: how absences are
   covered, the number of classrooms without a qualified teacher, an
   assessment?**
   - Does it reach the mid-career leaver at all, or only the retiree?

7. **Do you know about any other similar policy that aims to re-attract former
   teachers?**

8. **Overall, what is your assessment of its impact, and what will decide the
   next renewal?**

9. **Is there someone who has followed this closely you would recommend we
   speak to?**
