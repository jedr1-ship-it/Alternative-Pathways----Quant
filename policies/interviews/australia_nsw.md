# Australia — New South Wales
## Teachers Re-Engage, Department of Education (since November 2023)

**Who to ask:** the School Workforce directorate of the NSW Department of
Education, which handles attraction, retention and the placement of teachers in
public schools.

**What we know.** From November 2023 the Department went through its own records
of teachers who had left in the previous five years and approached more than
1,500 of them. By May 2024, 260 had expressed interest and 145 had joined the
pool of casual and temporary teachers serving more than 600 schools, about six
in ten of them retirees. Returners are helped with
accreditation, approval to teach and the working-with-children check, and
register on the casual booking platform schools use. A shorter re-accreditation
route for retired teachers appeared at the standards authority the month the
calls began. The press described the outreach as cold-calling; the Department has
not said what the channel was. No figures have been published since May 2024.

---

1. **How did this start?** Who first proposed going back to teachers who had
   already left, and who had to agree?
   - Was there a model, in Australia or elsewhere?

2. **How did you find the teachers you contacted, and how were they
   approached?**
   - Which records were used, and what had to be settled before former
     employees could be contacted?
   - Phone, letter, e-mail? What was said to them?
   - Everyone who had left, or a selection?

3. **What did they answer?** Refusals interest us as much as acceptances.
   - Did the refusals have anything in common?

4. **Once someone said yes, what followed, and where did it stall?**
   - The standards authority opened a shorter re-accreditation route for
     retired teachers the same month. Was that planned together?
   - Was the process itself changed, or was it the usual process with someone
     guiding them through?

5. **Who came back, and to what kind of post?**
   - Casual, temporary, permanent: did what they wanted match what schools
     needed?
   - Did they go where the shortage was, or where they lived?

6. **The pay rise and the reduction in administrative work came in at the same
   time. How much of the result do you attribute to the outreach itself?**

7. **Are the teachers who came back the same kind of teachers who leave?** What
   did they say about why they had left, and what had changed?
   - Is there a kind of leaver this approach does not reach?

8. **What became of them, and what did the exercise teach you?**
   - How many were still teaching a year later? Any permanent appointments?
   - Was it reviewed? Is it still running? What would you change?

9. **Is there someone else, in the Department or at the standards authority, we
   should speak to?**
