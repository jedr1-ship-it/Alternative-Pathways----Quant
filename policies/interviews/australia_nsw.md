# Australia — New South Wales
## Teachers Re-Engage, Department of Education (since November 2023)

**Who to ask for:** the School Workforce directorate of the NSW Department of
Education — the area that runs attraction, engagement and retention, and the
recruitment and placement of teachers for public schools.

**What we already know, so we do not ask it.** From November 2023 the Department
went through its own records of teachers who had left in the previous five years
and approached more than 1,500 of them; by May 2024, 260 had expressed interest
and 145 had been linked with schools, about sixty per cent of them out of
retirement. Returners are helped with accreditation, approval to teach and the
working-with-children check, and register on the casual booking platform the
schools use. The press described the outreach as cold-calling; the Department has
never said what the channel was. Nothing programme-specific has been published
since May 2024.

---

### Opening

1. Could you start by telling us how the idea came about? We are interested in
   the moment someone in the Department said, rather than advertise for
   teachers, let us go back to the ones who have already left.

2. Who had to be convinced, inside the Department or outside it, before it could
   start?

### How it was done

3. Going back to your own records to find former teachers is not something most
   systems do. How did you go about building that list, and what did you have to
   settle before you could contact people who no longer worked for you?

4. How were they approached, and what was said to them? We would like to
   understand what that first conversation was actually like.

5. What did people say back? We are as interested in the ones who said no as in
   the ones who said yes.

6. Once someone was interested, what happened next, and who accompanied them
   through it? Where did people get stuck?

7. Accreditation and the working-with-children check came up as the practical
   obstacles. Was the process genuinely changed for these teachers, or was it
   the same process with someone helping them through it?

### The teachers

8. Looking at the people who came back, who were they? What we can see is that
   about six in ten were retirees; we would like to hear about the rest.

9. What did they tell you about why they had left, and what made returning
   possible now and not before?

10. The Department has said that the pay rise and the cut in administrative work
    helped. From where you sat, how much of this was the outreach and how much
    was that everything else had changed?

11. What kind of work did they come back to, and did that turn out to be what
    they wanted?

### Results and what comes next

12. What happened to those teachers afterwards? We have never seen figures past
    May 2024.

13. Was any of this evaluated, formally or informally, and what did you conclude
    about whether it was worth the effort?

14. Where does the initiative stand now, and if you were starting again, what
    would you do differently?

### Close

15. Is there a colleague, in the Department or at the accreditation authority,
    who was close to this and could speak to the experience and the results? We
    would be very grateful for an introduction.
