# Australia — New South Wales
## Teachers Re-Engage, Department of Education (since November 2023)

**Who to ask for:** the School Workforce directorate of the NSW Department of
Education, which runs attraction, engagement and retention and places teachers
in public schools.

**What we already know, so we do not ask it.** From November 2023 the Department
went through its own records of teachers who had left in the previous five years
and approached more than 1,500 of them; by May 2024, 260 had expressed interest
and 145 had been linked with schools, about six in ten of them out of retirement.
Returners are helped with accreditation, approval to teach and the
working-with-children check, and register on the casual booking platform the
schools use; a streamlined re-accreditation route for retired teachers appeared
at the standards authority in the same month the calls began. The press described
the outreach as cold-calling; the Department itself has never said what the
channel was. Nothing programme-specific has been published since May 2024.

---

1. **How did the idea come about?** We are interested in the moment someone
   said: rather than advertise, let us go back to the teachers who have already
   left.
   - Who had to be convinced, inside the Department or outside it?
   - Was there a model you were looking at, here or elsewhere?

2. **Going back to your own records to find former teachers is something very
   few systems do. How did you build that list, and how were people
   approached?**
   - What did you have to settle before contacting people who no longer worked
     for you?
   - Was it a phone call, a letter, an e-mail — and what was actually said?
   - Was the list everyone who had left, or a selection?

3. **What did people say back?** We are as interested in those who said no as
   in those who said yes.
   - Did the reasons for saying no cluster around anything?
   - Did anyone say no at first and come round later?

4. **Once someone was interested, what happened next, and where did people get
   stuck?**
   - The accreditation route for retired teachers changed the same month. Was
     that designed alongside this, or did it happen to coincide?
   - Was the process really changed for these teachers, or was it the same
     process with someone walking them through it?

5. **Who came back, and into what?** We can see that about six in ten were
   retirees; we would like to hear about the rest, and about the work they
   took.
   - Casual, temporary, permanent — did the kind of work they wanted match what
     schools needed?
   - Did they go where the shortage was, or where they lived?

6. **The Department has said the pay rise and the cut in administrative work
   helped. From where you sat, how much was the outreach and how much was that
   everything else had changed?**

7. **When you think about why teachers leave this system, are the ones who came
   back the same people, or a different group?** This is the question our
   chapter turns on.
   - What did returners say about why they had left, and about what had changed
     for them?
   - Is there a kind of leaver this approach does not reach?

8. **What happened to them afterwards, and what would you say the initiative
   taught you?** We have never seen figures past May 2024.
   - Were any of the 145 still teaching a year later? Did any move to permanent
     posts?
   - Was it evaluated, formally or informally, and was it worth the effort?
   - Where does it stand now, and what would you do differently?

9. **Is there a colleague, in the Department or at the standards authority, who
   was close to this and could speak to the experience and the results?** We
   would be very grateful for an introduction.
