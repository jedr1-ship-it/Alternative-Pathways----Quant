# Australia — New South Wales
## Teachers Re-Engage, since 2023

**Who to ask:** the School Workforce directorate of the NSW Department of
Education.

**What we know.** From November 2023 the Department went through its own records
of teachers who had left in the previous five years and approached more than
1,500 of them. By May 2024, 260 had expressed interest and 145 had joined the
pool of casual and temporary teachers serving more than 600 schools, about six
in ten of them retirees. Returners are helped with accreditation, approval to
teach and the working-with-children check, and register on the casual booking
platform schools use. A shorter re-accreditation route for retired teachers
appeared at the standards authority the month the calls began. The press
described the outreach as cold-calling; the Department has not said what the
channel was. No figures have been published since May 2024.

---

1. **In what context did this initiative come about, and with what purpose?**
   Was the teacher shortage severe at the time?

2. **How did you find the teachers you contacted, and how were they
   approached?**
   - Which records were used, and what had to be settled before former
     employees could be contacted?
   - Phone, letter, e-mail? What was said to them?
   - Everyone who had left, or a selection?

3. **How would you describe the response you got?** Refusals interest us as
   much as acceptances.
   - Did the refusals have anything in common?

4. **We understand the programme offered help with re-accreditation and a
   placement in casual or temporary work. What did that help consist of in
   practice?**
   - The standards authority opened a shorter route for retired teachers the
     same month. Was that planned together?
   - Was the process itself changed, or was it the usual process with someone
     guiding them through?

5. **According to the sources we consulted, the initiative was launched right
   after a large pay rise and a cut in teachers' administrative workload. How
   much of the result do you attribute to the outreach itself, and how much to
   that?**

6. **The Department's media release of May 2024 says about 60 per cent of those
   who came back were retired. Is that right, and if so, why did they come
   back?**
   - And the other 40 per cent: who were they, and what had they been doing?
   - Did they go where the shortage was, or where they lived?

7. **Is there any record of how well the policy has worked — an internal
   evaluation, figures after May 2024?**
   - How many were still teaching a year later? Any permanent appointments?
   - Is the initiative still running?

8. **Overall, what is your assessment of its impact, and what has it taught
   you?**

9. **Is there someone else, in the Department or at the standards authority,
   you would recommend we speak to?**
