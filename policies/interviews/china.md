# China
## Silver-Age Lecturing Plan, since 2018

**Who to ask:** the Teacher Work Department of the Ministry of Education, and if
possible, a provincial education department that recruits for the plan.

**What we know:** Retired principals, senior teachers and 'special-grade'
teachers aged 65 or under are posted to rural primary and lower-secondary
schools in poor and remote counties, for at least one school year, where they
teach classes, coach the school's young teachers and advise the head on running
the school. They apply to a call issued by their province, keep their pension
and receive an allowance from the central government of 20,000 yuan a year
(about 2,500 euros) that covers a stipend, travel and insurance. The Ministries
of Education and Finance have run the plan since 2018, aiming at 10,000 posts in
its first three years, and recruited 7,000 teachers in the 2024 round. What we
do not know is how many have actually been recruited against those plans, how
long teachers stay, and whether anyone has assessed the effect on the receiving
schools.

1. **In what context did the plan come about in 2018, and with what purpose?**
   What was it meant to do that other measures for rural schools were not
   doing?
   - Why retired teachers, rather than more posts for young ones?

2. **How would you describe the path from application to a classroom in a
   distant county?**
   - Who decides which counties and schools receive a teacher?
   - What happens when applicants outnumber posts, or fall short of them?

3. **We understand that a silver-age teacher both teaches and works with the
   young teachers of the school. How is that balance struck in practice?**
   - What do the young teachers get from it?

4. **We understand that teachers receive an allowance on top of the pension,
   county housing and an agreement renewed each year. How was that arrangement
   arrived at, and does it hold?**
   - What does a county consider when it renews?
   - How long do teachers stay?

5. **Who applies, and why?**
   - What is hardest: distance from family, health, the gap between the schools
     they knew and the ones they arrive in?

6. **We noticed that the annual notices ask provinces to raise their completion
   rate. Is this information available? How big was the program's impact?**

7. **Is there any record of what the plan has changed in the receiving schools,
   for the pupils, for the young teachers, for whether they stay?**

8. **Overall, what is your assessment of the plan, and what should a country
   with an ageing teaching profession take from it, or avoid?**

9. **Is there someone at the ministry, or in a province that recruits for the
   plan, you would recommend we speak to?**
