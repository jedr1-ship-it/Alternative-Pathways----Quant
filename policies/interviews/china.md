# China
## Silver-Age Lecturing Plan (2018) and the National Silver-Age Teachers Action Plan (2023)

**Who to ask:** the Teacher Work Department of the Ministry of Education, which
issues the plan with the Ministry of Finance, and if possible a provincial
education department that recruits for it.

**What we know.** Since 2018 the Ministry of Education and the Ministry of
Finance have recruited retired principals, teaching-research officers,
special-grade and senior teachers, generally aged 65 or under, to county-town and
rural schools in formerly poor, border and minority counties. Recruitment is run
by provinces against named posts; the county education bureau signs an agreement
with the teacher, for at least one school year and renewed annually, on the
strength of a recent health certificate. The pension is untouched; the work is
funded at 20,000 yuan per person per year, shared between central and local
budgets, for an allowance, travel and accident insurance, and the county provides
housing. In 2023 ten government departments extended the model to higher,
vocational and private education, with a target of about 120,000 silver-age
teachers within three years. The annual plan for compulsory education has been
7,000 in 2024 and 2025, and the notices ask provinces to raise their completion
rate.

1. **How did the plan start in 2018, and what was it meant to do that other
   measures for rural schools were not doing?**
   - Why retired teachers, rather than more posts for young ones?
   - What changed in the thinking between 2018 and the 2023 plan?

2. **From application to a classroom in a distant county, how does it work?**
   - Who decides which counties and schools receive a teacher?
   - What happens when applicants outnumber posts, or fall short of them?

3. **What does a silver-age teacher do in the school week?**
   - How is the balance struck between teaching, mentoring young teachers and
     advising the leadership?
   - What do the young teachers get from it?

4. **An allowance on top of the pension, county housing, an agreement renewed
   each year: how was that arrangement arrived at, and does it hold?**
   - What does a county consider when it renews?
   - How long do teachers stay?

5. **Who applies, and why?**
   - What is hardest: distance from family, health, the gap between the schools
     they knew and the ones they arrive in?
   - What do those who leave early say?

6. **Is a silver-age teacher an answer to why teachers leave rural schools, or
   never go to them, or an answer to something else?**
   - Does their presence change whether young teachers stay?

7. **How has recruitment gone against the annual plan, and where is the
   difficulty?**
   - How is progress towards 120,000 measured, and where does it stand?
   - Has anyone looked at what the plan changed in the receiving schools?

8. **What should a country with an ageing teaching profession take from this,
   and what should it avoid?**
