# China
## Silver-Age Lecturing Plan, since 2018

**Who to ask:** the Teacher Work Department of the Ministry of Education, and if
possible a provincial education department that recruits for the plan.

**What we know.** The Silver-Age Lecturing Plan, run by the Ministry of
Education with the Ministry of Finance since 2018, posts retired principals,
teaching-research officers, special-grade and senior teachers, generally aged 65
or under, to county-town and rural schools in formerly poor, border and minority
counties. Provinces recruit against named posts; the county education bureau
signs a one-year agreement with the teacher, renewable, on the strength of a
recent health certificate; the pension is untouched, the work is funded at
20,000 yuan per person per year for an allowance, travel and accident insurance,
and the county provides housing. The plan aimed at 10,000 teachers in its first
three years and at 7,000 a year in 2024 and 2025; in 2023 ten government
departments extended the model to higher, vocational and private education, with
a target of about 120,000 silver-age teachers within three years. What we do not
know: how many have actually been recruited against those plans, since the
annual notices ask provinces to raise their completion rate, how long teachers
stay, and whether anyone has assessed the effect on the receiving schools.

1. **In what context did the plan come about in 2018, and with what purpose?**
   What was it meant to do that other measures for rural schools were not
   doing?
   - Why retired teachers, rather than more posts for young ones?
   - What changed in the thinking between 2018 and the 2023 plan?

2. **How would you describe the path from application to a classroom in a
   distant county?**
   - Who decides which counties and schools receive a teacher?
   - What happens when applicants outnumber posts, or fall short of them?

3. **We understand a silver-age teacher both teaches and works with the young
   teachers of the school. How is that balance struck in practice?**
   - What do the young teachers get from it?

4. **An allowance on top of the pension, county housing, an agreement renewed
   each year: how was that arrangement arrived at, and does it hold?**
   - What does a county consider when it renews?
   - How long do teachers stay?

5. **Who applies, and why?**
   - What is hardest: distance from family, health, the gap between the schools
     they knew and the ones they arrive in?
   - What do those who leave early say?

6. **We noticed that the annual notices ask provinces to raise their completion
   rate. How has recruitment gone against the plan, and where is the
   difficulty?**
   - How is progress towards 120,000 measured, and where does it stand?

7. **Is there any record of what the plan has changed in the receiving schools
   — for the pupils, for the young teachers, for whether they stay?**

8. **Overall, what is your assessment of the plan, and what should a country
   with an ageing teaching profession take from it, or avoid?**
