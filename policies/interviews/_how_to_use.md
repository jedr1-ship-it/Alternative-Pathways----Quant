# How to use these scripts

Each script is built for a conversation of forty-five minutes to an hour. It has
eight or nine open questions, in an order that goes from how the policy came
about to what it produced. Under most questions there are two or three probes:
they are not questions to be asked one after the other but things to reach for
if the answer stays on the surface.

The block at the start of each script, "what we already know", is for us, not for
them. It is there so we do not spend the interview on what is on the public
record, and so the person sees, from our first question, that we have read their
policy. Where it says "we understand" or "as far as we can tell", the point is
uncertain and worth confirming in passing.

Three things every interview should come away with, whatever the order: how the
policy came to exist and who decided its shape; what happened once it was
running, as told by someone who was there; and whether the people it brought
back are the people who leave. That last one is the question the chapter turns
on, and it is asked in each script in its own words.

Ask the closing question in every case. A second name is often worth more than
the first conversation.
