# Teacher Profession-Leaving Rates in the Seven CPF Countries

Cross-country synthesis for the countries of the Comparative Panel File (CPF), which harmonizes seven long-running household panels: PSID (US), BHPS/UKHLS (UK), SOEP (Germany), HILDA (Australia), KLIPS (Korea), RLMS-HSE (Russia), SHP (Switzerland). For each country, at least three distinct within-country sources/datasets were contrasted. All rates are annual percentages of the teacher stock unless noted. Compiled July 2026.

## Cross-country patterns

1. Attrition scales inversely with civil-service protection: Korea (quits 0.2–0.3) < Germany (quits 0.2–0.4) < Switzerland (definitive 1–2) < Scotland (~5; ~2 excl. retirement) < Russia (~4–6) < Australia (~5) < US (~8) < England (~9 sector exit).
2. In five of the seven countries the CPF household panel itself cannot support the estimate — the teacher subsamples of PSID, KLIPS, RLMS-HSE, SHP, and SOEP are too small, and no published panel-based rate exists. Where panel-based rates do exist (UKHLS 12.3; HILDA 11.6), they run roughly double the administrative benchmark, with documented occupation-coding churn as the mechanism. Household-survey occupational transitions overstate profession exit unless validated — which is exactly what the March CPS design (retrospective longest job vs. current occupation, validated 99.1% against panel classifications) addresses.
3. Definitions drive most of the cross-source spread within countries: gross school separations > sector exits > profession exits > definitive profession exits net of returns > voluntary quits. Every country ordering is preserved once definitions are aligned.
4. Intentions are not attrition anywhere: Australia 36–39% intend to leave vs ~5% do; Korea 87% have considered leaving vs 0.2–0.3% resign; Russia ~60% of young teachers want to leave their workplace vs ~6% early-career exit.

## United States (CPF panel: PSID)

Best estimate: ~7.5–8 percent per year, stable since ~2000.

| Source | Type | Definition | Years | Rate (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Learning Policy Institute (Carver-Thomas & Darling-Hammond 2017) | synthesis of SASS/TFS | leavers | 2011–2013 | ~8 |
